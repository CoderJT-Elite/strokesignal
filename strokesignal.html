Rehab begins when the brain gets an answer

Brain-computer interface rehab improves recovery because every attempt gets a response. strokesignal makes that loop something anyone can see and try, in a browser, in any clinic, school or living room.

Each of those twelve million strokes is a person whose hand stopped answering. This is what it looks like when it does.

The attempt is visible in the EEG

Imagining a hand movement quiets the rhythm over the motor area that runs that hand. The left hand is run from the right side of the brain (channel C4), the right hand from the left (C3).

A small model reads which hand

The model is standard: a band-pass filter at 8 to 30 Hz, common spatial patterns, then a linear classifier. It is trained on each session's calibration run and scored on a test run it never saw.

The answer arrives at once

When the model reads an attempt, the tendons draw and the fingers close, and a stimulation pulse runs as the amber line along the tendons. This is the moment the brain gets its answer.

Inside the signal

  1. The motor area still sends the command. The stroke cut the path to the hand, not the intent.
  2. Sixteen electrodes sit on the scalp and listen.
  3. An amplifier digitises every channel 256 times a second.
  4. C3 and C4 sit over the two motor areas. Imagining a hand movement quiets the rhythm on the opposite side.
  5. The model sees C4 go quiet and reads left hand. The arm answers.
